5What DDM can simulate: polycrystals in tension

Voronoi (24 grains), columnar (12 grains) and a single crystal under uniaxial tension; dislocations coloured by grain, with the stress–strain curves.

6Loop symmetry by orientation

Top views of the loops under the tip for [010], [101] and [111] indentation: 4-, 2- and 3-fold patterns, as Al indents along the same axes.

7Prismatic loops and pop-in

Al [101], R = 500 nm sphere. Loops nucleate one by one, cross-slip and close into prismatic loops along the loading axis (no artificial rule); the big pop-in comes once they build a dislocation forest.

8Cusping at a GB, as in MD

(100)|(310) symmetric tilt GB (18.43° about [010]), one Frank–Read source, R = 500 nm tip: half-loops bow into grain 2 along the GB and grow into new loops.

9Pile-up → transmission

Berkovich tip (R = 125 nm), symmetric [010] tilt GB of 8.7° at 194 nm from the tip axis: loops pile up behind the GB and their stress drives the emission into grain 2.

10Very close to a twist GB

Al [010], Berkovich tip: single crystal vs bicrystals with a 20° twist GB 89 nm from the tip axis, low vs high GB mobility; load–depth curve and dislocation density in grain 2.

12Pile-up thickens GB walls

Columnar polycrystal in tension, top view on grain 8 (walls of 31.6° and 26.4°): dislocations pile up at the walls and the dense layer grows into the grain.
