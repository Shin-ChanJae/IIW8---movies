Single crystals to polycrystals

Voronoi (24 grains), columnar (12 grains) and a single crystal under uniaxial tension; dislocations coloured by grain, with the stress–strain curves.

SINGLE CRYSTALS TO POLYCRYSTALS

Columnar: pile-up thickens the GB walls

Columnar polycrystal (12 grains) in tension, top view of grain 8 (GBs of 31.6° and 26.4°): dislocations pile up at the GBs and the dense layer grows into the grain.

NANOINDENTATION · SINGLE CRYSTAL

Prismatic loops and pop-in

Al [101], R = 500 nm sphere. Loops nucleate one by one, cross-slip and close into prismatic loops along the loading axis (no artificial rule); the big pop-in comes once they build a dislocation forest.

NANOINDENTATION · SINGLE CRYSTAL

Loop symmetry by orientation

Top views of the loops under the tip for [010], [101] and [111] indentation: 4-, 2- and 3-fold patterns, as Al indents along the same axes.

NANOINDENTATION · SINGLE SLIP SYSTEM BICRYSTAL

Cusping at a GB, as in MD

(100)|(310) asymmetric tilt GB (18.43° about [010]), one Frank–Read source, R = 500 nm tip: half-loops bow into grain 2 along the GB and grow into new loops.

NANOINDENTATION · BICRYSTAL

Pile-up → transmission

Pristine Al, Berkovich tip (R = 125 nm), symmetric [010] tilt GB of 8.7° at 194 nm from the tip axis: loops pile up behind the GB and their elastic interaction drives the transmission into grain 2.

NANOINDENTATION · BICRYSTAL

GB mobility shapes the first pop-in

Pristine Al [010], Berkovich tip (R = 125 nm): single crystal vs bicrystals with a 20° twist GB 89 nm from the tip axis, low vs high GB mobility (only the GB Mclimb differs); load–depth curve and dislocation density in grain 2.
